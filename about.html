About

Same loop, two settings

I've captained Cornell's Division I men's squash team since my junior year. Squash and robotics reward the same habit: try something, look honestly at what happened, adjust, and repeat quickly.

On the court

I was named captain as a junior after our team graduated eight seniors, so a young roster had to rebuild its standards fast. As captain I coordinate training and recruitment for more than 15 athletes. Before Cornell, I won gold for Team USA at the 2022 Pan American Junior Games.

In the lab

On my projects I run controlled comparisons, read the measured results instead of the hoped-for ones, and change one thing at a time. My GPU kernels went through four versions that way, and my LoRA project found a result I didn't expect because I measured it.

The loop

  1. 1. Try

    SquashRun a drill with one specific intention.

    RoboticsRun an experiment with one clear question.

  2. 2. Observe

    SquashWatch where the ball lands and review the match.

    RoboticsRead the measured result and the failure logs.

  3. 3. Adjust

    SquashChange one thing in the next game plan.

    RoboticsChange one variable in the next version.

  4. 4. Repeat

    SquashShort loops, every practice.

    RoboticsShort loops, every build.

The team I'm looking for

I work best with clear standards, direct feedback and short loops between trying something and seeing whether it worked. That's what a good squash team runs on, and it's what I want in an engineering team: small enough that everyone owns something real, and close enough to hardware that problems show up fast.
